Questions for your Pasadena cosmetic dentist

Ask your dentist to explain why one approach makes sense for you. Whitening changes tooth color, while veneers cover the front surfaces of selected teeth and may address certain concerns about shape or appearance. These treatments serve different purposes, so comparing them only by their finished look can leave out details that matter.

Before agreeing to a plan, ask:

  • Will this treatment involve removing any tooth structure?
  • How might existing crowns, fillings or veneers affect the result?
  • What can realistically change, and what will stay the same?
  • Will I need temporary restorations or additional appointments?
  • What maintenance should I expect afterward?

Preparing for a cosmetic dental consultation in Pasadena

Write down the specific things you notice about your smile. Perhaps a tooth looks shorter than its neighbor, or you'd prefer a different shade. Mention what you already like, too. That helps keep the conversation focused on changes you actually want.

Bring information about previous whitening, dental restorations and any sensitivity you've experienced. If you wear a retainer or night guard, ask whether you should bring it along. Reference pictures can help communicate preferences, but they aren't a promise that the same appearance will suit another person's mouth.

Tell the dentist about upcoming events and your availability. Treatment timing depends on the examination and the work involved, so discuss your schedule before making plans around a finished result.

Mistakes to avoid with cosmetic dental treatment

Don't assume the brightest shade will be your favorite once you see it beside your other teeth. Discuss color choices under normal conditions and ask how treated teeth could compare with existing dental work. Whitening does not change the color of crowns or veneers the way it changes natural teeth.

Another mistake is treating appearance as a separate issue from comfort. Mention tenderness, tooth sensitivity or discomfort when chewing during your consultation. Those concerns may affect what your dentist recommends addressing first.

Questions people ask about Cosmetic dentist Pasadena

Should I choose whitening or veneers?

That depends on what you'd like to change and the condition of your teeth. Whitening addresses tooth color, while veneers may address selected concerns involving tooth shape and appearance.

Can I whiten teeth if I already have crowns?

Ask your dentist to assess the crowns and surrounding natural teeth before starting. Crowns won't lighten like natural enamel, which can affect how evenly the shades match.

What should I tell the dentist about my preferred result?

Describe the features you'd like adjusted and anything you'd prefer to keep. You can bring reference photos, but the dentist will need to explain what's appropriate for your own teeth.
