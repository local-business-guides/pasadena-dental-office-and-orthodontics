What should adult clear aligner treatment include?

Clear aligner treatment uses a series of removable trays to move teeth in planned stages. A provider should first assess your teeth, gums, bite, and treatment goals, then explain whether aligners are a suitable option or if another approach may work better.

If treatment goes ahead, ask how the plan is created, how progress is checked, and what happens if a tray stops fitting as expected. Your care should also include a discussion of retainers after active treatment, since teeth can shift if you don't follow a retention plan.

What will a Pasadena professional check at the first visit?

A careful evaluation starts with your dental and medical history, an exam of your teeth and gums, and a look at how your upper and lower teeth meet. The provider may recommend photographs, scans, or X-rays when needed to understand tooth position and plan care. Ask what records are being taken and what each one is meant to show.

The provider should also ask about crowns, implants, missing teeth, gum concerns, jaw discomfort, and past orthodontic treatment. These details can affect which movements are appropriate and how predictable aligners may be. If you have untreated decay or gum disease, those concerns may need attention before tooth movement begins.

How long might adult aligner treatment take?

Timing depends on how far teeth need to move, the bite, how consistently you wear the trays as directed, and whether the plan needs adjustment. A provider should give you an estimate based on your exam rather than promise a fixed finish date before seeing your teeth.

Ask how often progress visits are expected and what could extend treatment, such as missed wear time, a tray that doesn't fit, or the need for additional aligners. Get instructions for what to do if a tray is lost or damaged instead of trying to change the sequence on your own.

What mistakes should you avoid before starting?

  • Choosing on the advertised fee alone without checking what follow-up, refinements, and retainers cost or include.
  • Starting before a clinician checks for cavities, gum problems, or other dental concerns that may need care first.
  • Assuming every bite or tooth-position concern can be corrected with aligners. Ask the provider to explain limits and alternatives for your specific case.
  • Skipping progress visits or changing trays outside the schedule you were given.
  • Forgetting to ask who to contact if your teeth hurt more than expected, a tray won't seat, or your bite feels different.

Before you agree, make sure you understand the written plan, the expected check-ins, and what happens if treatment isn't progressing as planned. You should feel comfortable asking for an explanation in everyday language.

When should you call a professional?

Arrange an evaluation if crowded or spaced teeth, a changing bite, or difficulty cleaning between teeth is bothering you. Contact a dental professional promptly about ongoing tooth or gum pain, bleeding, swelling, or a damaged tooth rather than treating those issues as an aligner question alone.

During treatment, reach out to the provider if an aligner doesn't fit as directed, breaks, or causes a problem that doesn't settle. Don't force a tray into place or skip ahead without instructions from the team responsible for your care.
